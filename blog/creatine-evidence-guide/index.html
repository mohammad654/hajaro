Creatine: What the Research Says

Creatine is one of the most researched sports supplements. Here is what it can and can't do, without the marketing.

By Coach Hajaro · Published August 8, 2026 · 5 min read

On this page

  1. What creatine does
  2. Typical dosage
  3. What it will not do

Supplements are the smallest piece of the puzzle. Training, food and sleep come first. That said, creatine monohydrate is one of the most studied supplements in sports nutrition.

What creatine does

Creatine fills up your muscles' stores of phosphocreatine, which helps them make energy for short, hard efforts like heavy sets. Over time this can help you train a little better, and so gain a little more muscle and strength.

Typical dosage

A common intake is 3 to 5 g of creatine monohydrate a day, at any time of day. A loading phase is optional; it only fills your muscles faster.

What it will not do

  • It won't replace consistent training or good food.
  • It does not burn fat.
  • The effects are small.
Supplements are not medicines and are not meant to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease. Talk to a healthcare professional before using supplements, especially if you have a medical condition or take medication.
